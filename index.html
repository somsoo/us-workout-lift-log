📖 Strength Principles: 1RM and Progressive Overload

Effective strength and hypertrophy adaptations rely on quantified progressive overload—gradually increasing training resistance, repetitions, or total session volume over time (National Strength and Conditioning Association - NSCA):

  • The Epley Formula for 1RM: 1RM = Weight × (1 + Reps ÷ 30). This equation provides high statistical accuracy for submaximal efforts between 1 and 10 repetitions without risking the neurological exhaustion of a true maximum lift.
  • Volume Load Calculation: Calculated as Sets × Reps × Weight. Tracking session volume ensures systematic stimulus progression week-over-week.
  • Rate of Perceived Exertion (RPE): Based on the Repetitions in Reserve (RIR) system: RPE 10 corresponds to 0 reps in reserve; RPE 9 equals 1 rep in reserve; RPE 8 equals 2 reps in reserve.

Scientific references: National Strength and Conditioning Association (NSCA) Essentials of Strength Training & Conditioning; Epley B. (1985) Poundage Chart; American College of Sports Medicine (ACSM) Resistance Training Guidelines.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

How is One Rep Max (1RM) estimated?

The calculator implements the clinically validated Epley equation: 1RM = Weight × (1 + Reps ÷ 30). For sets between 1 and 10 repetitions, this provides a reliable projection of maximum single-effort strength.

How is total workout volume calculated?

Workout volume load is calculated as the sum of (Weight × Reps) across all completed sets in the session. Tracking volume load is fundamental for ensuring progressive overload over time.

How does the rest timer operate?

Clicking a quick rest preset (60s, 90s, 120s, or 180s) starts an in-browser countdown. A gentle auditory notification alerts you when your recovery window is complete.

Are my workout logs stored or shared?

No. All workout logs and training session histories are saved exclusively in your browser local storage. No fitness data or personal logs are uploaded to any external server.

⚠️ Exercise Safety Disclaimer

Weightlifting and resistance training carry inherent risks of musculoskeletal strain or injury. Perform appropriate dynamic warm-ups, utilize proper barbell safety collars and spotters, and consult a qualified fitness professional or sports physician prior to attempting maximal training loads.
